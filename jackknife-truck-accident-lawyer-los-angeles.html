Jackknife truck crashes

When a trailer swings across the highway, the carrier's maintenance records tell the story.

A jackknife occurs when a truck's trailer swings out of alignment with the cab, rotating 90 degrees or more and sweeping across multiple lanes of traffic. The physics are straightforward: when the tractor's drive-axle brakes lock or engage harder than the trailer brakes, the tractor decelerates faster than the trailer behind it. The trailer's momentum pushes it forward and to the side, pivoting around the fifth wheel coupling until the rig folds like a closing jackknife. Vehicles in adjacent lanes have no warning and no escape path.

The most common cause is improper brake adjustment — specifically, an imbalance between the tractor and trailer braking systems. Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration regulations under 49 CFR § 396.3 require carriers to systematically inspect, repair, and maintain all commercial vehicles, including regular brake inspections with specific adjustment standards. Carriers that defer brake maintenance to cut costs create exactly the mechanical conditions that cause jackknife crashes. Anti-lock braking systems on trailers — federally mandated since 1998 — are designed to prevent wheel lockup, but only if they are properly maintained and functional.

Jackknife crashes also result from blown tires that destabilize the trailer, improperly loaded cargo that shifts weight under braking, and wet or icy road surfaces that reduce traction. But even when road conditions contribute, the carrier's obligation to maintain brakes, tires, and ABS systems in working order remains. A properly maintained truck with functional ABS, correctly adjusted brakes, and a properly loaded trailer should not jackknife under conditions the driver could reasonably encounter.

Under California's pure comparative fault rule (Civil Code § 1431.2), your recovery is reduced only by any fault attributable to you — it is never eliminated entirely. Step-by-step

What to do after a jackknife crash.

Jackknife crashes involve commercial carriers with legal teams that activate immediately. The maintenance records and black box data that prove negligence can disappear if you don't act fast.

1
At the scene
Call 911 and request a full accident investigation.

Jackknife crashes involving commercial trucks typically trigger a more detailed police investigation than a standard collision. Get the responding officer's name, badge number, and report number. If CHP responds, they will usually conduct measurements and document the trailer's final angle relative to the cab — critical evidence for reconstruction.

2
At the scene
Photograph the truck, trailer angle, and all vehicles involved.

Document the angle of the trailer relative to the cab, the position of all vehicles struck, skid marks, debris field, road surface conditions, and any visible damage to the truck's tires or braking components. The trailer's final resting angle often indicates whether the jackknife was caused by tractor brake lockup versus trailer swing.

3
Within hours
Get an attorney involved before the carrier's team arrives.

Trucking companies dispatch rapid-response teams — sometimes within hours — to the crash site. These teams work for the carrier, not for you. Their job is to control the narrative and manage evidence. Having your own attorney send a spoliation letter immediately preserves the black box data, ELD records, brake maintenance logs, and driver qualification files before anything is altered or lost.

4
Same day
Get emergency medical treatment and follow through.

Jackknife crashes often involve multi-vehicle pile-ups with high-force impacts. Even if you walked away from the scene, internal injuries, spinal compression, and traumatic brain injuries can present with delayed symptoms. A same-day medical evaluation creates the causation record linking your injuries to this specific crash.

5
Within days
Do not give a recorded statement to the carrier's insurer.

The trucking company's insurer will contact you quickly, often framing it as a routine call. Their goal is to get statements on record that can be used to shift blame — suggesting you were in the truck's blind spot, changed lanes suddenly, or could have avoided the trailer. Let your attorney handle all communication with the carrier and its insurer.

6
Ongoing
Keep all medical records and document lost income.

Jackknife crash injuries often require extended treatment — surgeries, rehabilitation, and specialist follow-ups over months or years. Every medical visit, prescription, therapy session, and day of missed work builds the damages case. Keep a file of everything and share it with your attorney regularly.

What jackknife impacts cause

A trailer sweeping lanes at
highway speed is catastrophic.

Jackknife crashes involve an 80,000-pound rig folding across traffic. Vehicles are struck broadside by the trailer with no warning. The injuries reflect that force.

Traumatic Brain Injury
Multi-vehicle pile-ups caused by a jackknifed trailer produce secondary and tertiary impacts — a vehicle struck by the trailer is often pushed into other vehicles or barriers. These repeated forces cause concussion, diffuse axonal injury, and more severe TBI even in belted occupants.
Spinal Cord Injuries
A broadside trailer impact compresses the passenger compartment laterally, transmitting force directly through the seat and body. Vertebral fractures, disc herniation, and spinal cord damage can result in partial or complete paralysis requiring lifelong care and adaptive equipment.
Crush Injuries
When a trailer sweeps into a vehicle's path, the massive weight difference causes catastrophic compartment intrusion. Occupants can be trapped by collapsed roof pillars, doors, and dashboard components, leading to crush syndrome, compartment syndrome, and the need for emergency extrication.
Multiple Fractures
The multi-directional forces in a jackknife pile-up — initial trailer impact, secondary vehicle collisions, and barrier strikes — produce complex fracture patterns across multiple body regions. Femur, pelvis, rib, and upper extremity fractures frequently occur together, requiring multiple surgeries and extended rehabilitation.
Internal Organ Damage
Blunt abdominal trauma from vehicle deformation during a jackknife impact can rupture the spleen, liver, or kidneys, or cause mesenteric tears and internal hemorrhaging. These injuries are frequently not immediately apparent and require emergency imaging to diagnose before they become life-threatening.
Burns
Jackknife crashes carry an elevated fire risk: ruptured fuel tanks on the tractor or trailer, friction-ignited fires from the trailer dragging across pavement, and — in hazmat loads — chemical exposure. Burn injuries require specialized treatment centers and often result in permanent disfigurement.
What they don't want you to know

The carrier's insurer has a playbook. So do we.

Trucking companies carry large insurance policies and experienced defense teams. Here's what they typically argue in jackknife cases — and how we dismantle each defense.

Tactic 01
"Road conditions caused the jackknife — the driver braked properly"
Carriers routinely blame wet roads, ice, or sudden obstacles to deflect from their own maintenance failures. Brake maintenance records often tell a different story — deferred inspections, out-of-adjustment brakes, and non-functional ABS systems are the actual mechanical conditions that cause a trailer to swing under braking a competent driver should be able to handle.
Tactic 02
"Your vehicle was in the truck's path — contact was unavoidable"
This argument attempts to normalize a catastrophic equipment failure as an unavoidable accident. A properly maintained truck with functioning ABS, correctly adjusted brakes, and a properly loaded trailer should not jackknife under normal braking conditions. The truck created the hazard — your position on the road did not.
Tactic 03
"Multiple vehicles were involved — liability is shared"
In multi-vehicle pile-ups, carriers try to spread fault across all drivers involved to reduce their exposure. The truck that jackknifed created the hazard that swept across lanes — other vehicles were victims of that hazard, not contributors to it. Accident reconstruction consistently shows the jackknife as the initiating event.
Tactic 04
"The trailer was within legal weight limits"
Being under the maximum gross weight doesn't mean the cargo was loaded safely. Even within weight limits, improper distribution — heavy cargo loaded high or shifted to one side — raises the center of gravity and creates instability under braking or lane changes. Loading records and weight distribution documentation often reveal the real problem.

Questions & answers

Jackknife truck crash FAQ.

Sudden hard braking is the most common trigger, but the underlying cause is usually mechanical — improper brake adjustment where the tractor brakes engage harder than the trailer brakes, causing the trailer to swing out. Other causes include blown tires that destabilize the trailer, wet or slick road surfaces reducing traction, improperly loaded cargo that shifts weight suddenly, and anti-lock braking system (ABS) failures on the trailer. FMCSA regulations require regular brake inspections and specific adjustment standards, and carriers that defer this maintenance create the conditions for a jackknife.
Liability typically falls on multiple parties. The truck driver may be liable for excessive speed or improper braking technique. The motor carrier is often liable for deferred brake maintenance, unrealistic scheduling that pressured the driver, or failure to ensure the truck passed pre-trip inspections. Third-party brake maintenance contractors can be liable if they performed substandard work. Tire manufacturers may be liable if a defective tire caused the loss of traction. And the cargo loading company may bear responsibility if improper weight distribution made the trailer unstable under braking.
Brake inspection and maintenance records are the single most important category of evidence — they show whether the carrier kept the brakes in proper adjustment and whether inspections were performed on schedule or deferred. Electronic logging device (ELD) and black box data reveal the truck's speed and braking events in the seconds before the jackknife. ABS system maintenance logs show whether the trailer's anti-lock braking system was functional. Pre-trip inspection reports — which drivers are required to complete before every trip — often reveal a pattern of checked boxes without actual inspections. Accident reconstruction and road surface condition reports round out the evidence.
Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration regulations (49 CFR § 396.3) require carriers to systematically inspect, repair, and maintain all commercial vehicles, including regular brake inspections with specific adjustment standards. Carriers that defer brake maintenance to reduce costs — or that falsify inspection records to appear compliant — face direct liability for negligence. When maintenance records show a pattern of missed or delayed brake inspections, it establishes that the carrier knew or should have known the brakes were not roadworthy. This evidence can also support claims for punitive damages if the deferral was willful.

All victims benefit from the same core liability evidence: the maintenance records, black box data, and accident reconstruction that prove the jackknife was caused by carrier negligence.
