California wrongful death law

Two claims. One lawsuit. Maximum recovery for your family.

California law provides two separate legal mechanisms for families who have lost someone to another's negligence. A wrongful death claim (CCP § 377.60) compensates the surviving family — spouses, children, domestic partners — for what the death took from them: financial support, companionship, guidance, and the presence of the person they lost. A survival action (CCP § 377.30) is filed by the estate and covers what the deceased would have recovered had they survived: their own pain and suffering, medical expenses before death, and lost income.

Both claims can be filed in the same lawsuit. In fatal accident cases, the combination of wrongful death damages and survival action damages produces the largest personal injury recoveries in California. We retain economists and life-care planners who calculate the full economic value of what your family lost — present-value lifetime earnings, replacement cost of household services, and comprehensive life-care projections for surviving dependents.

The insurance and legal teams on the other side are working the case from the moment the death is reported. They are investigating the circumstances of the death, preserving evidence favorable to them, and looking for arguments to limit what they pay. Your family needs representation that matches their effort immediately.

Under California's pure comparative fault system (Civil Code § 1431.2), even if the deceased contributed to the accident, the family can still recover — reduced only by the percentage of fault attributed to the deceased. If a government vehicle, entity, or employee was involved in the death, a government tort claim must be filed within six months. Call us today.

What wrongful death takes from a family

Every loss your
family has a right to pursue.

California law allows recovery for both economic and non-economic losses. We retain economists, life-care planners, and vocational experts to quantify every category.

Loss of Financial Support
Present-value calculation of all income the deceased would have earned over their expected working life, based on employment history, career trajectory, education, and industry earnings data. This is the largest economic component for most families.
Loss of Companionship & Love
The loss of the deceased's society, comfort, attention, and affection. Non-economic, but real and fully compensable. Judges and juries treat these damages seriously.
Loss of Parental Guidance & Training
Minor children have a specific right to recover for the loss of the parental guidance, training, education, and mentorship the deceased would have provided. Expert testimony on the value of parenting is standard in these cases.
Loss of Household Services
The market replacement cost of all household services the deceased provided — cooking, cleaning, childcare, home maintenance, yard work, financial management. Calculated using Bureau of Labor Statistics wage data and expert economic testimony.
Funeral & Burial Expenses
All reasonable funeral, burial, or cremation costs, including funeral home services, cemetery fees, grave marker, flowers, catering for services, and travel costs for immediate family members.
Pre-Death Pain & Suffering (Survival)
If the deceased survived the incident for any period of time, their estate can recover damages for the pain and suffering they experienced before death. This is the survival action claim filed by the estate under CCP § 377.30.
Deceased's Lost Earnings Before Death
Income the deceased would have earned between the date of the accident and the date of death. For deaths that occur days or weeks after the injury, this amount can be substantial — and is part of the survival action.
Medical Expenses Before Death
All emergency care, hospitalization, surgery, and medical treatment costs incurred between the date of injury and the date of death. Survival action damages recoverable by the estate regardless of whether insurance paid them.
Bystander Emotional Distress
Surviving family members who were present at or near the scene of the death may have separate negligent infliction of emotional distress (NIED) claims. This is separate from wrongful death damages and can significantly increase total recovery.
What defendants argue — and how we counter it

They will minimize your
family's loss. We quantify it.

Defendants and their insurers fight wrongful death cases hard. Here are the arguments they make — and how we prepare to defeat each one.

Tactic 01
"Liability is disputed — your loved one contributed to the accident."
California follows pure comparative fault. A defendant who is 20% at fault owes 20% of the damages — they do not escape liability entirely. Defendants routinely overstate decedent fault to reduce their exposure. We counter with accident reconstruction and regulatory violation evidence.
Tactic 02
"Pre-existing health conditions caused or contributed to the death."
The eggshell plaintiff rule applies to wrongful death as to all torts. A defendant who negligently harms a person takes that person as they find them — prior heart condition, prior injury, age. The defendant is responsible for the full extent of the harm their negligence caused, including any fatal consequence that a healthier person might have survived.
Tactic 03
"The economic damages calculation is speculative."
Economic damages are supported by expert testimony from forensic economists, vocational rehabilitation specialists, and life-care planners. These experts calculate present-value lifetime earnings using actuarial tables, wage data, and the deceased's specific employment history. The calculation is standard economic methodology that courts accept routinely.
Tactic 04
"Non-economic damages — grief, companionship — are excessive."
Non-economic damages in wrongful death cases — loss of companionship, society, guidance — are real, recognized by California law, and taken seriously by juries. We present them with testimony from surviving family members, expert witnesses on the value of family relationships, and mental health professionals. These are the damages that juries remember.
Tactic 05
"The statute of limitations has expired."
Two years from the date of death for private defendants. Six months for government defendants. Defendants occasionally argue tolling does not apply. We file well within any arguable deadline and calendar every filing obligation from the date of hire.
Tactic 06
"Here is a settlement offer — you should take it and move on."
Early settlement offers in wrongful death cases are designed to resolve the case before the family has expert economic analysis or full understanding of every compensable loss. The value of a wrongful death case is almost always greater than initial offers reflect. We do not accept offers until we have a complete picture.
