Personal injury in Los Angeles

California gives you two years. Insurers start working against you on day one.

Los Angeles County records more traffic injuries than any other county in California. The I-405, I-10, US-101, and I-110 corridors are among the most crash-dense freeways in the nation. Surface streets through South LA, East LA, and Koreatown see injury rates that consistently rank among the state's highest. When crashes happen here, the consequences are severe — and the insurance aftermath is complicated.

The other party's insurer assigned an adjuster to your claim within 24 hours of your injury. That adjuster's only job is to close your case for as little as possible. They will call you, seem sympathetic, offer a recorded statement, and make an offer that sounds fair but isn't. Do not speak with any insurer before speaking with us.

Law Dog is based in Downtown Los Angeles at 515 Flower Street. We represent injured Angelenos across every neighborhood — from Woodland Hills to Watts, from Hollywood to Harbor City. We work on pure contingency: no fee unless we win, no upfront costs, no risk to you. Our interests and yours are identical: maximize your compensation.

California's two-year statute of limitations under CCP § 335.1 applies to most personal injury claims. Claims against the City of Los Angeles, LADOT, Metro, or CalTrans must be filed within just six months under Government Code § 911.2. Contact us today — the earlier we start building your case, the more evidence we can preserve.

Yes. California follows pure comparative fault under Civil Code § 1431.2. Even if you were 40% responsible, you recover 60% of your total damages. Insurance companies routinely inflate victim fault percentages to cut payouts — it is one of their primary negotiation tactics. Government entity claims require a formal administrative tort claim within six months of the incident under Government Code § 911.2. This applies to LAPD vehicles, LADOT vehicles, Metro buses and trains, and injuries caused by dangerous City-maintained road conditions. The six-month deadline is absolute — courts cannot extend it. If you were injured by a government vehicle or on a poorly maintained City road, call us immediately.
