California catastrophic injury law

These injuries require a different kind of case.

A catastrophic injury fundamentally alters a person's life — their ability to work, to move independently, to maintain relationships, to experience the world. The legal case must reflect that reality. Insurers and defendants know this, and their opening strategy is to dispute the severity of the injury, challenge the need for future care, and pressure for an early settlement before the full picture emerges.

We build catastrophic injury cases from the ground up with the expert team these cases demand: board-certified treating physicians, independent medical evaluators, certified life care planners, vocational rehabilitation specialists, and forensic economists. The life care plan is the backbone of the damages case — a comprehensive, expert-authored document projecting every medical and support cost the client will incur for life. Without it, insurers make offers that cover past expenses but ignore future reality.

The critical mistake in catastrophic injury cases is settling before maximum medical improvement (MMI) is reached. Permanent impairment takes time to define. We advise clients to follow all medical recommendations, document every consequence of the injury, and not accept any offer until the full lifetime cost is established and documented. An early offer in a catastrophic case is almost always a fraction of what is owed.

We advance all litigation costs — expert fees, medical record compilation, depositions, filing costs — and collect only from the recovery. If we don't win, you owe nothing. Call us to review your case.

Six steps that protect catastrophic injury cases.

What you do in the first weeks after a catastrophic injury determines what evidence survives, what coverage is available, and how strong the lifetime damage case will be.

Independent claims available to spouses and domestic partners for loss of companionship, affection, and household assistance. Also available to parents of injured minors. A separate, significant damage category insurers routinely undervalue.
What insurers do in catastrophic cases

Every major insurer has
a playbook for these cases.

When the exposure is large, the defense gets sophisticated. Insurers deploy independent medical examiners, hire vocational specialists to minimize future care needs, and challenge every element of the lifetime damage model. Here is what they do — and how we respond.

Tactic 01
"Your pre-existing condition caused most of this."
Defense IME doctors attribute current impairment to pre-existing conditions. California's eggshell plaintiff doctrine holds defendants responsible for the full extent of injury, even if a pre-existing condition made the plaintiff more vulnerable. We document the baseline before the accident and the change caused by it.
Tactic 02
"You contributed to your own injury — comparative fault."
Defendants inflate the plaintiff's fault percentage to reduce the payout. California's pure comparative fault rule means even 40% responsibility still allows 60% recovery. Given the size of lifetime damages, 60% of a properly documented lifetime model still vastly exceeds a low early offer. We fight fault allocation with accident reconstruction experts.
Tactic 03
"Your injuries aren't as severe as claimed." (DME)
Defense medical examiners — hired to minimize, not diagnose — conduct brief examinations and produce reports contradicting treating physicians. We respond with treating physician testimony, independent specialist evaluations, neuropsychological testing, and functional capacity assessments. A defense IME contradicting years of treating physician documentation rarely survives cross-examination.
Tactic 04
"Future damages are speculative — we don't owe them."
Insurers argue future medical needs, care costs, and lost income are too speculative to award. California law allows full recovery of future damages proven to a reasonable certainty. A certified life care planner and forensic economist convert future projections into provable damages.
Tactic 05
"Medical causation is disputed — your injury had another cause."
Defense experts challenge whether the accident caused the specific injury — particularly for TBI and disc herniations that don't appear on initial imaging. We document the gap between pre-incident health and post-incident function with medical records, employment records, family testimony, and neuroimaging. MRI findings that appear at 3 months are still caused by the accident.
Tactic 06
"Here's a quick settlement — sign before you know the real cost."
Early settlement offers are the most dangerous tactic in catastrophic cases. They come before MMI, before the life care plan is complete, before forensic economic analysis is done. Signing a release extinguishes all future claims regardless of how costs escalate. We never recommend settlement until the full lifetime cost is established and documented.

Filing deadlines

Two years. Six months for government. The clock starts at injury.

Two years from injury under CCP § 335.1 for claims against private defendants. Six months under Government Code § 911.2 for claims against government entities — municipal vehicles, public transit, government property. For injured minors, the two-year clock typically begins at age 18. Evidence also vanishes fast: surveillance footage is overwritten in days, black box data in the next crash. Retaining counsel early preserves evidence while it still matters.

Frequently asked

Questions people ask us about catastrophic injury cases.

California law does not define "catastrophic injury" in a single statute, but courts consistently treat spinal cord injuries, traumatic brain injuries, amputations, severe burns, vision or hearing loss, anoxic brain injuries, and polytrauma as catastrophic because of their permanent, life-altering nature. The legal significance is that these cases require a dramatically different damages framework — lifetime cost projections, life care plans, and forensic economic analysis rather than simple past-expense tallying.
A life care plan is a comprehensive, expert-authored document that projects every medical and support cost a catastrophically injured person will incur for the rest of their life — surgeries, medications, in-home care, adaptive equipment, home modifications, vocational therapy, and psychological support. Without a life care plan, insurers make low offers based on past expenses only. With a certified life care planner and supporting medical expert testimony, we can demand and prove the full lifetime cost of the injury.
The general statute of limitations is two years from the date of injury under CCP § 335.1. If the defendant is a government entity — LADOT, a public school, a transit authority — you have only six months to file a government tort claim under Government Code § 911.2. For injured minors, the two-year clock typically starts at age 18. Evidence preservation is critical from day one — waiting even weeks can result in lost surveillance footage, destroyed black box data, and fading witness memory.
Possibly. When the at-fault party's insurance is insufficient to cover lifetime care needs, we investigate every avenue of additional recovery: the injured person's own underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage, umbrella policies, employer coverage (if the at-fault driver was working), property owner coverage (if the injury occurred on someone's premises), and product liability claims (if defective equipment contributed). For catastrophic cases, identifying all layers of coverage is essential to building an adequate recovery.
Loss of consortium is a separate legal claim available to the spouse or registered domestic partner of a catastrophically injured person. It compensates for the loss of companionship, affection, sexual relations, and assistance with household activities. Parents of injured minor children also have a consortium-equivalent claim in California. These are independent damages, separate from the injured person's own claim, and can add significant value to a catastrophic injury case.
Defendants routinely hire defense medical examiners (DMEs) — doctors paid to minimize injury severity. We counter with the treating physicians' records and testimony, independent neurological and orthopedic evaluations, neuropsychological testing for TBI, and life care planner testimony linking injury findings to lifelong needs. A defense IME that contradicts years of treating physician documentation rarely survives cross-examination.
Lost earning capacity is calculated by a forensic economist using the injured person's work history, education, vocational trajectory, and the degree of permanent impairment. Unlike lost wages (past income missed during recovery), lost earning capacity covers the full projected difference between what the person would have earned over their working lifetime and what they can now earn given their permanent limitations. For young or high-earning clients, this is often the largest single component of economic damages.
If you already signed a release, you generally cannot pursue additional recovery — which is exactly why insurers push low early settlement offers before the full scope of a catastrophic injury is known. If you have not yet signed anything, do not accept any settlement offer in a catastrophic case without consulting a catastrophic injury attorney first. Early offers in these cases are almost always a fraction of actual lifetime costs.
California's pure comparative fault rule (Civil Code § 1431.2) allows you to recover even if you were partially at fault — your damages are reduced by your percentage of responsibility but not eliminated. Even if you were 40% at fault, you can still recover 60% of your full damages. Given how large lifetime damages are in catastrophic cases, 60% of a proper lifetime damage model still vastly exceeds 100% of an improperly low early offer.
Most catastrophic injury cases take one to three years to fully resolve, and some take longer. The primary reason is that the full scope of permanent impairment — and the full cost of lifetime care — cannot be accurately established until maximum medical improvement (MMI) is reached. Settling too early, before MMI, risks locking in compensation that doesn't reflect the true lifetime cost of the injury. We guide clients through the entire timeline and ensure no resolution happens before the damage picture is fully documented.
