Multi-vehicle accident

The more defendants there are, the harder each one fights to blame the others.

In a two-car crash, there are two parties. In a five-car pile-up, there are five parties — each represented by their own insurer and attorney, each incentivized to shift fault to every other driver. California's pure comparative fault system (Civil Code § 1431.2) apportions liability among all responsible parties. This is actually beneficial to plaintiffs — you can recover from each defendant proportionate to their fault — but it requires building a complete liability picture that clearly establishes each defendant's share.

Multi-vehicle accidents require reconstruction. Physical evidence — gouge marks, debris fields, paint transfer, final rest positions, skid patterns — must be documented before it disappears. Traffic and surveillance camera footage from overpasses, CalTrans cameras, and nearby businesses captures what police reports often miss. Telematics data from modern vehicles — speed, steering, braking, throttle in the seconds before impact — is available through subpoena or preservation letters and must be sought immediately.

Each defendant has their own auto insurance policy. A victim in a multi-vehicle crash may have claims against 3, 4, or more separate policies — plus their own uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage if any defendant is underinsured. We identify every available insurance source and pursue each one simultaneously. An insurer that quickly pays their insured's policy limits can sometimes be released; others must be litigated aggressively.

Vehicles are repaired or scrapped. Cameras overwrite footage. Witnesses' memories fade. Black box data has limited onboard storage. In multi-vehicle crashes, the window to capture the full evidentiary picture is measured in days, not weeks. Contact us immediately — we dispatch accident reconstruction specialists to preserve evidence before it is gone.

Step-by-step

What to do after a multi-vehicle crash.

The actions taken in the first 72 hours after a multi-vehicle crash have an outsized effect on your recovery — both medical and financial. Each step below is specific to the unique challenges pile-ups create.

Free case review →
1
At the scene
Call 911 and stay put — but get clear of traffic.

In multi-vehicle crashes on freeways, secondary crashes are common — move to the shoulder or barrier wall if possible, activate hazard lights, and position yourself away from traffic. Note every vehicle involved: make, model, color, license plate if visible. Take photos of every vehicle's position before anyone moves them, even if police haven't arrived.

2
At the scene
Collect every witness's contact information before they leave.

In a 5-car pile-up, a driver two cars back who saw the initiating event is your most valuable witness. Their account is independent of all defendants and corroborates your reconstruction. Most people will leave the scene within 30 minutes — get names and phone numbers before they do.

3
Within 24 hours
Issue preservation letters to every defendant.

Each identified defendant must receive a written demand to preserve their vehicle's EDR/black box data, dash camera footage, telematics data, cell phone records (distracted driving), and any recorded communications. We file these immediately upon retention. Failure to preserve after demand is spoliation — a serious evidentiary problem for the party who destroys evidence.

4
Within 48 hours
Engage an accident reconstruction expert.

Multi-vehicle crashes require reconstruction that goes beyond the police report. Physical evidence at the scene deteriorates rapidly. We retain qualified reconstructionists who can document gouge marks, debris scatter patterns, and vehicle crush data before CalTrans sweeps the scene and vehicles are moved or repaired.

5
As each defendant is identified
File a coordinated lawsuit naming all defendants.

Each defendant must be separately served with process. Their insurers must be separately notified. Each insurance company will attempt to conduct its own investigation favorable to its insured. We control the narrative by filing a coordinated lawsuit that names all defendants, preventing each insurer from running a separate shadow investigation.

6
Through litigation
Anticipate and dismantle the coordinated defense playbook.

Multi-vehicle cases often involve coordinated defense: defendants' insurers share expert witnesses, align on depositions, and present a unified "someone else did it" front. We anticipate this playbook and build our case to be credible against every defense theory simultaneously — allocating clear fault percentages to each defendant supported by reconstruction, black box data, and eyewitness testimony.

Common injuries

What a pile-up
actually does.

Multi-vehicle crashes produce high-energy, multi-directional forces on occupants. We know which specialists to retain and how to document what insurance adjusters dismiss as minor.

Whiplash & Cervical Spine Injury
Chain-reaction rear impacts at freeway speeds cause high-energy cervical loading. Multiple impact sequences — being hit from behind and then into the car in front — can produce bi-directional cervical injury that is more severe than single-impact whiplash. These injuries are frequently undervalued in multi-vehicle cases because "multiple low-speed impacts" sounds minor. It is not.
Traumatic Brain Injury
Sequential impacts in a pile-up can produce TBI even where no single impact would. Acceleration-deceleration forces from multiple collisions are additive. "Mild" TBI from a multi-vehicle crash that disrupts cognitive function and ends a professional career is a catastrophic economic loss — and our neuropsychological documentation proves it.
Thoracic & Lumbar Spine Fractures
Compression loading in multi-vehicle crashes commonly fractures thoracic and lumbar vertebrae. Burst fractures, compression fractures, and transverse process fractures are seen at freeway speeds. These injuries require surgical intervention in severe cases and produce lasting impairment of mobility, lifting capacity, and sustained physical activity.
Rib Fractures & Pneumothorax
Seat belt-restrained occupants in high-energy multi-vehicle crashes often suffer multiple rib fractures. Rib fractures alone are intensely painful and limit breathing, coughing, and movement for 6–8 weeks. Associated pneumothorax requires chest tube placement. Flail chest — multiple adjacent fractures — is a life-threatening emergency requiring mechanical ventilation.
Lower Extremity Fractures
Feet, ankles, tibias, and femurs are commonly injured when the vehicle cabin is compromised in pile-up crashes. Surgical fixation with hardware, extended non-weight-bearing recovery, and risk of post-traumatic arthritis are typical consequences. Lower extremity fractures affecting walking and standing have significant vocational impact in labor-intensive occupations.
Psychological Trauma & PTSD
Multi-vehicle pile-up survivors report elevated rates of PTSD, particularly when the crash involved fire, entrapment, or witnessing other victims' injuries. PTSD from a major traffic event is a recognized, treatable, and compensable diagnosis. Phobia of freeway driving, which affects many survivors, can itself have significant vocational consequences in a sprawling auto-dependent city like Los Angeles.
What they don't want you to know

The playbook they run on multi-vehicle victims.

When multiple insurers are involved, their strategies multiply. Knowing what's coming is the first defense.

Tactic 01
The "Other Driver Did It" Defense
Each defendant's insurer funds a defense designed to put fault on every other driver. In a 5-car pile-up, this produces 5 competing narratives, each exonerating its insured. The plaintiff is caught in the crossfire. We counter by building an independent reconstruction case that assigns clear, evidence-backed fault percentages — letting the defendants fight each other while the reconstruction evidence speaks for itself.
Tactic 02
Lowball Early Settlement From One Insurer
When one defendant is clearly most at fault, their insurer may offer early policy limits to get out of the case before punitive or shared liability is established. Accepting one defendant's early settlement can complicate claims against other defendants under joint and several liability rules. We evaluate every settlement offer in the context of the full liability picture before advising acceptance.
Tactic 03
Attacking Causation in Sequential Impacts
Insurers argue that their insured's impact was minor or occurred after initial injuries were already sustained — making them responsible for "aggravation only," a smaller damages exposure. We counter with biomechanical expert testimony that establishes the specific contribution of each impact to the total injury, and with reconstruction data showing force vectors and delta-V at each collision.
Tactic 04
Coordinated Defense Expert Strategy
Insurers in multi-defendant cases frequently retain the same accident reconstructionist, biomechanical expert, and defense medical examiner across all defendants. This presents an aligned expert front that, if unchallenged, can significantly undercut the liability case. We identify these coordination patterns through discovery and depose each expert on the scope of their retention and communications with co-defendants' counsel.

Questions & answers

Multi-vehicle accident
FAQ.

California follows pure comparative fault under Civil Code § 1431.2. Each defendant is liable for their proportionate share of your damages. If Driver A is 60% at fault and Driver B is 40% at fault, you recover 60% of your damages from Driver A and 40% from Driver B. You do not need a single "primary" defendant — each is independently liable for their share. This system benefits plaintiffs because multiple smaller policies can collectively exceed what a single policy would provide.
One lawsuit, all defendants. We file a single civil complaint naming every responsible party — each served separately, each represented by their own insurer and attorney. This consolidates the litigation into one proceeding where all the evidence is before one court, prevents inconsistent rulings on the same facts, and allows us to enforce cross-claims between defendants that often surface more evidence of individual fault.
Your own auto insurance policy's uninsured motorist (UM) coverage applies. UM coverage steps in for any defendant who was uninsured or whose policy limits are insufficient to cover your damages. In a multi-vehicle crash, UM and underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage can stack on top of recovery from insured defendants. We identify every available insurance source — defendants' policies plus your own UM/UIM — before making any coverage decisions.
Through biomechanical expert analysis. A qualified biomechanical engineer can analyze the direction, magnitude, and timing of each impact relative to the injury mechanism — establishing which collision caused which injury, or quantifying the contribution of each impact to the total harm. This expert testimony is essential in multi-vehicle cases where defendants argue "someone else's impact caused the real damage."
Subrogation means your insurer may have a right to recover from your settlement the amounts they paid for your medical bills, rental car, or other covered losses. California law limits subrogation rights in personal injury cases — your insurer generally cannot recover until you've been "made whole" (fully compensated). We track all subrogation claims and ensure they don't improperly reduce your net recovery.
Longer than single-defendant cases, typically. Coordinating discovery across multiple defendants, resolving coverage disputes between competing insurers, and deposing multiple parties and their experts adds time. Expect 18–36 months for a contested multi-vehicle case. However, cases where fault is clear and reconstruction is strong can resolve at mediation — often 12–18 months post-filing. We give you an honest timeline based on the specific defendants and evidence in your case.
Time limit

Two years to file. The evidence disappears in days — preserve it now.

California Code of Civil Procedure § 335.1 gives you two years from the date of injury to file a personal injury lawsuit against each defendant. But the real urgency in multi-vehicle cases is immediate: accident reconstruction evidence at the scene has hours or days before CalTrans clears it. Camera footage is overwritten within days. Vehicle EDR data is overwritten if the vehicle is operated after the crash. We send preservation letters and retain reconstruction experts within 24–48 hours of retention. If a government vehicle or road defect contributed, the deadline is six months under Government Code § 911.2. Call today.
