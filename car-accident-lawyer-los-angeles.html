Car accidents

California gives you two years. Insurance companies start working against you on day one.

California roads are among the most congested in the country. Millions of daily commuters, aggressive drivers, distracted rideshare operators, and commercial trucks share infrastructure pushed well past capacity. When something goes wrong, the consequences are severe — and the aftermath is complicated.

The other driver's insurer assigned an adjuster to your claim within 24 hours of the crash. That adjuster's job — their only job — is to close your case for as little as possible. They will call you, seem sympathetic, request a recorded statement, and make an offer that sounds reasonable but isn't. Do not speak with them before speaking with us.

Law Dog represents car accident victims on pure contingency. We advance every litigation cost — accident reconstruction, biomechanical experts, medical record compilation, depositions — and collect only as a percentage of what we recover. No recovery means no fee, period. Our interests and yours are identical: maximize your compensation.

California's pure comparative fault rule (Civil Code § 1431.2) means you can recover even if you were partly at fault — your recovery is simply reduced by your share of responsibility. And under Government Code § 911.2, claims against public entities (city buses, dangerous road conditions maintained by CalTrans or LADOT) must be filed within just six months. Immediately after

What to do after a crash.

The first hour after an accident determines more of your case than you realize. Evidence is time-sensitive — skid marks fade, cameras overwrite, memories blur. Follow these steps exactly.

Tell us what happened →
1
At the scene — immediate
Don't move unless there's danger

If it's safe to stay put, do. Moving vehicles before police arrive can destroy critical evidence about point of impact. Turn on your hazards, move yourself to safety if needed, but preserve the scene for the report.

2
At the scene — immediate
Call 911. Get a police report.

Even for minor crashes, an official police report is foundational to your claim. Get the responding officer's name and badge number, and request a copy of the report. Without one, insurers will dispute everything.

3
At the scene — immediate
Document everything, right now

Photograph all vehicle damage, the road, skid marks, traffic signs, traffic lights, weather conditions, and any visible injuries — yours and others'. If there are witnesses, get their names and phone numbers. Record the other driver's license, plate, insurance card, and registration.

4
Same day — critical
See a doctor today. Not tomorrow.

Adrenaline suppresses pain — you may feel fine and have serious injuries. A gap between the crash and your first medical visit becomes a gap in your claim. Insurers will argue your injuries weren't caused by the crash. Go to an ER, urgent care, or your doctor the same day.

5
Within 24 hours
Do not talk to any insurance adjuster

The other driver's insurer will call you quickly — they're trained to. Do not give a recorded statement. Do not accept a check. Do not say "I'm okay." Anything you say will be used to minimize your claim. Tell them your attorney handles all communications, then call us.

6
As soon as possible
Call Law Dog. Start building the case.

Evidence disappears fast. Intersection cameras overwrite in 24–72 hours. Skid marks fade within days. The at-fault driver's insurer is already preserving evidence in their favor. We send preservation letters, retain investigators, and begin documenting before anything else is lost.

Common injuries from car accidents

The injuries insurers
try hardest to minimize.

Every injury below has been undervalued by insurance adjusters who argue it's "soft tissue only" or "pre-existing." We document, argue, and litigate each one.

Whiplash & Cervical Spine
The most frequently dismissed injury — and one of the most debilitating. Chronic neck pain, limited range of motion, and radiating nerve pain that can last years. Requires MRI documentation and specialist opinions.
Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI)
Concussions and mild TBIs don't always show on initial CT scans but cause months of cognitive impairment, headaches, and personality changes. Neuropsychological testing is essential to valuing these cases.
Herniated & Bulging Discs
Disc injuries in the cervical and lumbar spine cause severe radiating pain, numbness, and weakness. They often require epidural injections or surgical intervention — cases worth substantially more than soft-tissue claims.
Broken Bones & Fractures
Ribs, wrists, clavicle, pelvis, and femur fractures from airbag deployment, dashboard impact, or side intrusion. Surgery, hardware, and extended rehabilitation all factor into your damages calculation.
Spinal Cord Injuries
Partial or complete spinal cord injuries cause permanent neurological deficits. These are seven-figure cases requiring life-care planning, vocational experts, and long-term medical projections.
Soft Tissue & Nerve Damage
Sprains, strains, torn ligaments, and peripheral nerve injuries may not appear on imaging but cause real, ongoing functional limitations. We use treating physician testimony and functional capacity exams to prove the impact.
Internal Injuries
Liver lacerations, spleen ruptures, and internal bleeding are common in high-speed or side-impact crashes. Symptoms may be delayed — another reason to go to the ER the same day as any significant collision.
Psychological Trauma
PTSD, driving phobia, anxiety, and depression after a serious crash are compensable as non-economic damages. Psychiatric and psychological records support these claims in litigation and at settlement.
Wrongful Death
When a crash kills a family member, California's wrongful death statute (Code of Civil Procedure § 377.60) allows surviving family to recover economic loss, loss of companionship, and funeral expenses. These cases are handled with gravity and full resources.
What adjusters don't tell you

How insurance companies close cases cheap. And what we do about it.

Insurance adjusters are professionals whose performance is measured by how little they pay on each claim. Every tactic below has been used against Law Dog clients — before they called us.

Tactic 01
The fast call and the friendly voice
Adjusters call within 24–48 hours — sometimes within hours — of the crash. They seem sympathetic, ask how you're feeling, and offer to "help get this resolved quickly." The goal is a recorded statement. Anything you say about your injuries, your pain level, or what happened will be used to establish a baseline that minimizes your claim. "I'm a little sore" becomes the ceiling on your injury value.
Tactic 02
The quick-pay offer before you know your injuries
A check for $2,500 or $5,000 arrives within days of the crash, before you've finished treatment — often before you've fully understood your injuries. It comes with a release that permanently closes your claim. Disc herniation that requires surgery six weeks later? Already released. Signs a settlement release before fully healed is one of the most expensive mistakes injury victims make.
Tactic 03
Disputing or inflating your percentage of fault
California's comparative fault rule means the insurer directly profits from assigning you more of the blame. If they can argue you were 40% at fault instead of 0%, they cut your payout by 40%. They do this routinely, with little evidence. Our job is to build the factual record that defeats those arguments — police reports, witness statements, video, accident reconstruction if needed.
Tactic 04
The "independent" medical examination
Insurers request an IME — supposedly a neutral medical opinion — conducted by a physician the insurer pays repeatedly. Studies consistently show IME doctors reach favorable-to-insurer conclusions at rates far above treating physicians. We prepare clients for these exams, retain our own expert witnesses, and challenge IME opinions that contradict treating physician findings.
Tactic 05
Surveillance and social media monitoring
If your case has value, the insurer may hire investigators to follow you and monitor your social media for evidence that your injuries are exaggerated. A photo from a family event, a check-in at a gym, a single caption can be used to challenge your claim. We advise every client on protective measures from day one.
Tactic 06
Delaying until the statute runs out
In multi-year cases, some insurers deliberately slow-walk negotiations with the goal of running out the two-year statute of limitations. When time expires, your right to sue is gone permanently. We calendar every deadline from day one and file suit if necessary to preserve your rights — regardless of where settlement talks stand.

What you can recover

Three categories of damages. We pursue all of them.

California allows car accident victims to recover the full spectrum of damages caused by another driver's negligence. Here's what that means in practice.

Category 01
Economic Damages
All medical bills — past
ER, hospitalization, surgery, specialists, imaging, PT, chiropractic
All medical costs — future
Projected treatment, surgery, medications, and long-term care if injury is permanent
Lost wages
Every day of work missed because of the crash or recovery
Lost earning capacity
Future income you can no longer earn due to permanent limitations
Out-of-pocket expenses
Transportation to medical appointments, home care, modifications to home or vehicle
Category 02
Non-Economic Damages
Pain and suffering
Physical pain — past, ongoing, and future — caused by the crash and injuries
Emotional distress
Anxiety, PTSD, depression, driving phobia, and psychological trauma
Loss of enjoyment of life
Activities, hobbies, and relationships you can no longer participate in
Disfigurement and scarring
Visible scarring or permanent physical changes from the crash
Loss of consortium
Impact on your relationship with spouse or domestic partner — a separate recoverable element
Category 03
Property & Special Damages
Vehicle repair or replacement
Full fair-market value if totaled; full repair cost if repairable — not the insurer's depreciated estimate
Rental car costs
Transportation while your vehicle is being repaired or replaced
Diminished vehicle value
A repaired car is worth less than an undamaged one — this "diminution of value" is separately recoverable
Personal property damage
Phones, glasses, car seats, equipment damaged in the crash
Punitive damages
Available when conduct is egregious — DUI crashes, deliberate acts, gross recklessness

Statute of limitations

California gives you two years.
Government crashes: six months.

Under California Code of Civil Procedure § 335.1, you have two years from the date of the crash to file suit. Once that window closes, it cannot be reopened — no exceptions for late discovery of injuries, financial hardship, or delay by the insurer.

If a government vehicle, a city bus, a CalTrans road condition, or a public facility was involved, Government Code § 911.2 requires a formal administrative claim within six months. This is an absolute bar — courts cannot waive it.
