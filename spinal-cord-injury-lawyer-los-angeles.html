Spinal cord injury

The most expensive injury in personal injury law. Treated like a number by insurers.

SCI affects approximately 17,000 Americans per year. Motor vehicle crashes are the leading cause, followed by falls, violence, and sports. A cervical injury — C1 through C7 — can produce tetraplegia (all four limbs), requiring 24-hour attendant care, power wheelchair, ventilator support in complete high-cervical injuries, and $1–5 million in lifetime care costs. Even an incomplete lumbar injury changes employment, mobility, and daily function permanently.

Insurers treat catastrophic injury cases as financial liabilities to minimize. Their standard playbook: deploy a defense medical examiner who disputes injury severity, challenge the need for future care items, argue that less costly interventions are "reasonable," and make a one-time offer that sounds large but covers only a fraction of lifetime costs. A $2 million offer for a 30-year-old quadriplegic who will need $400,000 per year in care is not justice.

Law Dog builds SCI cases with a team: a life care planner who projects every cost over the client's actuarial lifespan, a vocational rehabilitation expert who quantifies lost earning capacity, treating physiatrists and neurologists, and where necessary, an economist to present future damages in present-value terms. The case we present to a jury or in settlement demand reflects the full financial reality of living with SCI.

Evidence in SCI cases is time-sensitive. Accident reconstruction, vehicle black box data, and surveillance footage must be preserved immediately. Treating hospitals must be engaged to ensure records are comprehensive. And life care planning must begin before the insurer's team establishes a competing narrative. Step-by-step

What to do after a spinal cord injury.

The decisions made in the first days after an SCI — medical, legal, and evidentiary — have a lasting impact on both functional outcomes and the value of your case. Follow these steps in order.

Free case review →
1
At the scene
Do not move a potential SCI victim.

If there is any possibility of spinal injury, do not move the person unless there is an immediate threat to life (fire, flooding). Spinal movement before immobilization can convert an incomplete injury to a complete one. Call 911 and keep bystanders back. This single decision can affect outcomes for a lifetime.

2
At the hospital
Request a complete spinal imaging workup.

Emergency trauma evaluations are lifesaving but not always comprehensive for SCI documentation. Advocate for full MRI of the entire spine, not just the primary injury level. Secondary injury levels are common in high-energy crashes and may be missed in initial triage — these missed levels become critical evidence and affect the long-term damages picture.

3
Within 48 hours
Preserve the accident vehicle.

Do not authorize repair or release of the vehicle to an insurance company or towing yard. The vehicle's Event Data Recorder (black box) captures pre-crash speed, braking, steering, and seatbelt data. This data is critical evidence that can establish the force of impact and the at-fault driver's actions. We move to preserve it immediately upon retention.

4
As soon as possible
Engage a spinal cord injury rehabilitation center.

The quality of early rehabilitation has lasting impact on functional outcomes. Major SCI rehab centers in the Los Angeles area have specialized teams. Your attorney can help coordinate rapid access to top-tier facilities — which also creates the treating team that becomes your expert witnesses. The stronger your treating team, the stronger your case.

5
Before any insurer contact
Do not speak to any insurance adjuster without counsel.

In SCI cases, adjusters move quickly to take recorded statements and make early offers before the full picture of lifetime costs is known. A premature settlement of a spinal cord injury case is one of the most catastrophic legal mistakes a family can make. Once a release is signed, no additional recovery is possible — ever. Call us before you speak to anyone.

6
Immediately
Begin life care planning.

A certified life care planner builds the roadmap of every cost you will face: attendant care hours, equipment replacement cycles, home modifications, therapy, medications, specialist visits, and hospitalizations over your life expectancy. This document drives the value of your case. The earlier it begins, the stronger it is — and the harder it is for the defense to challenge.

SCI classifications

The injury levels.
The lifetime costs.

Each classification of spinal cord injury carries a distinct functional picture and a distinct damages framework. We know what every level of injury costs — and we document every dollar.

Complete Tetraplegia (C1–C4)
Loss of all motor and sensory function below the neck. Mechanical ventilation may be required for high-cervical injuries. 24-hour attendant care, power wheelchair, modified vehicle, and extensive home modification are baseline requirements. Lifetime care costs routinely exceed $5 million. These are the largest personal injury verdicts in California.
Incomplete Tetraplegia (C5–C8)
Partial preservation of motor or sensory function. Varying degrees of hand, arm, and upper body function remain. Power or manual wheelchair typically required. Life care costs remain in the millions — and "incomplete" does not mean "recovered." Functional plateau typically occurs 12–18 months post-injury.
Complete Paraplegia (T1–L2)
Loss of motor and sensory function in the lower extremities. Upper body strength and hand function preserved. Manual wheelchair typically used. Employment impact varies — many paraplegics return to work in modified roles. Lost earning capacity, home modification, and attendant care remain significant damage categories.
Incomplete Paraplegia
Partial lower extremity function. Some ambulatory capacity may be preserved with assistive devices. Neuropathic pain, bowel/bladder dysfunction, and sexual dysfunction are common chronic complications that significantly affect quality of life — and are independently compensable under California law.
Cauda Equina Syndrome
Injury to the nerve roots below the spinal cord. Can result from disc herniation, fracture, or compression. Presents with bowel/bladder dysfunction, saddle anesthesia, and lower extremity weakness. Surgical decompression is urgent — delayed treatment worsens prognosis. When caused by a traumatic event, full tort damages apply.
Chronic Pain & Secondary Conditions
SCI survivors face cascading secondary conditions: pressure ulcers (requiring hospitalization), urinary tract infections, autonomic dysreflexia, respiratory complications, depression, and chronic neuropathic pain. These secondary costs are part of the damages case and are routinely undervalued in initial settlement discussions.
What they don't want you to know

The playbook they run on SCI victims.

Insurance companies follow a coordinated strategy to limit catastrophic injury payouts. Knowing what's coming before it arrives is the first line of defense.

Tactic 01
The Lowball Life Care Plan
Insurance companies hire their own life care planners who consistently project lower care costs, shorter life expectancy, and fewer needed services than treating physicians recommend. The difference between the defense life care plan and plaintiff's can be millions. We retain nationally recognized life care planners with documented SCI expertise to rebut these projections comprehensively.
Tactic 02
The "Future Medical Is Speculative" Defense
Insurers argue that future medical costs are too uncertain to award with precision. California law expressly allows recovery of future medical expenses based on reasonable probability — not certainty. We present a detailed, physician-supported life care plan that meets the legal standard and leaves nothing to speculation. This argument fails when the case is built correctly from the start.
Tactic 03
Dispute of Injury Severity
Defense medical examiners frequently dispute injury classification, overstating residual function and understating impairment. A one-time examination by a paid defense expert carries far less weight than years of treating records from specialized SCI rehabilitation teams. We ensure your treating physicians are properly engaged as expert witnesses — not sidelined while a hired examiner shapes the narrative.
Tactic 04
Early Settlement Before Prognosis Is Clear
The first 12–18 months after SCI are a period of functional change. Insurers push hard for early settlement before the injury plateau is established. A settlement that looks adequate at 3 months may be catastrophically insufficient once the full permanency of the injury is confirmed. We do not recommend settlement until maximum medical improvement is established and the life care plan is finalized.

Complete cervical injuries with lifetime care needs routinely produce verdicts and settlements in the $5–15 million range. California follows pure comparative fault (Civil Code § 1431.2). Your recovery is reduced by your percentage of fault but not eliminated. We fight every comparative fault allegation rigorously.
