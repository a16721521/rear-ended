Rear-end collisions

The most common crash in California. The most undervalued injury claim.

Rear-end collisions account for roughly 29% of all crashes in California and produce the most disputed personal injury claims in the state. The reason: the car often looks fine. Modern bumpers are engineered to absorb and redirect energy — protecting the vehicle. The occupant's body absorbs what the bumper doesn't. At closing speeds as low as 5 mph, the cervical spine can sustain injury that takes years to fully manifest.

The other driver's insurer knows this. They deploy a coordinated strategy from the moment they learn the collision was low-speed: delay your medical treatment, dispute the mechanism of injury, and make an early offer before you know what you're dealing with. The offer that sounds reasonable today may be a fraction of what your care will actually cost over the next two years.

Under California Vehicle Code § 21703, a driver must not follow another vehicle more closely than is "reasonable and prudent." When someone hits you from behind, that presumption of negligence is on their side — not yours. The burden is on them to explain why they didn't stop.

Step-by-step

What to do after a rear-end crash.

The actions you take in the first 72 hours after a rear-end collision have an outsized effect on your recovery — both medical and financial. Follow these steps in order.

Free case review →
1
At the scene
Stay put and call 911.

Even if it seems minor, a police report is foundational to any claim. It establishes the date, time, location, parties, and often a preliminary fault determination. Without it, the other insurer will dispute the facts of the crash itself.

2
At the scene
Photograph everything before moving the vehicles.

Both vehicles from multiple angles, the damage to both bumpers, the road surface, the relative positions, any skid marks, and the intersection or freeway lane markings. Also photograph your own body — bruising from the seat belt, airbag burns, or neck position at impact all become relevant.

3
At the scene
Do not say "I'm fine."

Adrenaline and cortisol are powerful pain suppressors. Many clients walk away from rear-end crashes feeling shaken but functional, then wake up two days later unable to turn their head. Any statement that you're uninjured — to the other driver, to the responding officer, or on social media — will be used against you. The only thing you owe anyone at the scene is your insurance and contact information.

4
Within 24 hours
Seek medical attention, even if you feel okay.

See a doctor the same day or next morning. Tell them about the crash, describe what happened to your body (the jolt, the direction of impact, any immediate sensations), and let them document everything. A gap in treatment — even a 48-hour gap early in the case — is one of the insurance adjuster's most potent weapons. Consistent medical records from day one close that door.

5
Within 48 hours
Don't repair your vehicle yet.

Your vehicle damage — or the relative lack of it — is evidence. A biomechanical engineer may need to examine the bumper deformation, the crush pattern, or the data from the airbag control module (black box). Once the car goes to the body shop, that evidence is gone. Photograph everything first; let us advise on timing before you authorize repairs.

6
Before you speak to anyone
Call us before you give any statement.

The other driver's insurer will call — sometimes within hours. They'll be friendly, express concern for your wellbeing, and ask to take a recorded statement. Decline politely and call us first. Recorded statements given before an attorney is involved consistently produce reduced settlement offers. We handle all insurer communication on your behalf once retained.

Common injuries

What the impact
actually does.

Rear-end injuries are frequently invisible on initial imaging but devastating in daily function. We know which specialists to retain and how to document what insurance adjusters dismiss.

Whiplash / Cervical Strain
The hallmark rear-end injury. The head snaps backward then forward faster than the muscles can brace. Grade I–III cervical sprains affect ligaments, tendons, and nerve roots. Chronic whiplash-associated disorder can last years.
Herniated or Bulging Discs
Compression forces drive disc material into the spinal canal, pressing on nerve roots. C4–C7 herniations from rear-end crashes cause radiating arm pain, numbness, and weakness — and frequently require injections or surgery.
Traumatic Brain Injury
The brain moves inside the skull on impact. Concussion, post-concussion syndrome, and diffuse axonal injury can all result from rear-end crashes — even without head contact. Cognitive symptoms, memory gaps, and light sensitivity are common.
Facet Joint Injury
Cervical facet joints bear the rotational stress of a rear-end impact. Facet arthropathy and joint inflammation cause chronic neck pain that doesn't show on standard imaging — requiring medial branch block injections to diagnose and treat.
Thoracic Outlet Syndrome
Seatbelt-restrained rear-end occupants frequently sustain brachial plexus stretching. TOS presents as arm tingling, weakness, and vascular symptoms — and is chronically misdiagnosed, delaying treatment and complicating claims.
Psychological Injury / PTSD
Being struck without warning produces acute stress responses in many victims. PTSD, driving anxiety, and sleep disturbance are compensable under California law as non-economic damages — and we document them with the same rigor as physical injuries.
What they don't want you to know

The playbook they run on rear-end victims.

Insurance adjusters follow a script designed to minimize rear-end payouts. Knowing what's coming is the first defense.

Tactic 01
The Low-Impact Defense
"Your car barely has a scratch — you couldn't have been seriously hurt." This is the most pervasive rear-end claim defense in California. It is also routinely rejected by juries when confronted with biomechanical engineering testimony. Modern bumpers protect the car, not the occupant. We retain engineers who testify to exactly this point.
Tactic 02
The Fast Early Offer
Within days of the crash — before your diagnosis is complete and before your treatment has begun — the adjuster calls with a settlement figure. It sounds reasonable. It is almost certainly a fraction of your actual damages once surgery, physical therapy, lost wages, and pain and suffering are calculated. Once you sign a release, the case is permanently closed. Never settle without legal review.
Tactic 03
Pre-Existing Condition Gambit
If your medical records show any prior neck or back treatment — even decades ago — the insurer will argue your current symptoms are pre-existing and unrelated to the crash. California's eggshell plaintiff doctrine says otherwise: you are entitled to compensation for aggravation of a pre-existing condition. We document the baseline and prove the delta.
Tactic 04
Delayed-Onset Denial
"You didn't seek treatment immediately, so you must not have been injured." Delayed onset is one of the most documented features of whiplash — not evidence of a fabricated claim. We prepare clients for this argument before it arrives and counter it with medical literature and expert testimony on pain physiology.

Time limit

Two years from the crash. Not one day more.

California Code of Civil Procedure § 335.1 gives you two years to file a lawsuit. If the at-fault vehicle was a government vehicle — a city bus, a public works truck, a CalTrans vehicle — you have just six months to file an administrative claim under Government Code § 911.2.
