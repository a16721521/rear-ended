Injured because someone else was careless? California law is on your side — if you act in time.

A serious injury upends everything at once: your health, your income, your family's stability. While you're in treatment, the at-fault party's insurance company is already working the file — recording statements, disputing fault, and calculating the smallest number you might accept. You should never negotiate against professionals alone.

Law Dog represents injured people on pure contingency. We front every cost of building your case — accident reconstruction, medical experts, depositions — and we collect a fee only as a percentage of what we recover for you. Under California law, you're generally entitled to recover the full cost of your medical care (past and future), lost wages and earning capacity, and compensation for pain, suffering, and the ways the injury has changed your life. Because California follows pure comparative fault, you can recover even if you were partly responsible — your award is simply reduced by your share of fault. But the clock is short: most claims must be filed within two years, and claims against government entities within just six months.

If you've been injured

Five moves that protect your case.

What you do in the first days after an accident can change your recovery by six figures. Here's the playbook.

01

Get medical care immediately — even if you feel fine

Adrenaline masks injuries. Brain trauma, internal bleeding, and soft-tissue damage often surface days later. See a doctor within 24 hours and follow every treatment recommendation: gaps in care are the first thing insurers use to argue you weren't really hurt.

02

Document everything at the scene

Photos of vehicles, hazards, road conditions, and your injuries. Names and phone numbers of witnesses. The police report number. If a store or property is involved, ask that an incident report be created — and get a copy. Evidence that isn't captured in week one often disappears forever.

03

Don't give the insurance company a statement

The other side's adjuster will call quickly, sounding friendly, asking for "just a recorded statement." You are not required to give one. A casual "I'm fine, thanks" can be quoted back at you months later to deny your claim. Politely decline and refer them to your attorney.

04

Preserve records — and stay off social media

Keep every bill, receipt, pay stub, and medical record. Start a daily note about pain levels and what the injury prevents you from doing. And assume the insurer is watching your social accounts: one smiling photo can be twisted into "evidence" you've recovered.

05

Talk to a lawyer before you sign anything

Early settlement offers are calculated to close your file before the full cost of your injury is known — future surgeries, lost earning capacity, long-term care. Once you sign a release, it's over. A free consultation costs you nothing and can't hurt your case. Waiting can.

What you can recover

Compensation covers more than the ER bill.

California law entitles injury victims to three categories of damages. Insurers calculate the first, lowball the second, and hope you never learn about the third.

Economic damages

The countable losses

Every dollar the injury has cost you — and will cost you in the future.

  • Emergency and ongoing medical care
  • Future surgeries & rehabilitation
  • Lost wages while you recover
  • Diminished earning capacity
  • Property damage
  • Home care & household services

Non-economic damages

The human losses

Often the largest part of a serious-injury recovery — and the part insurers fight hardest to minimize.

  • Pain and suffering
  • Emotional distress & anxiety
  • Loss of enjoyment of life
  • Disfigurement and scarring
  • Loss of consortium
  • Permanent disability

Punitive damages

The punishment

Available when the defendant's conduct was malicious or recklessly indifferent — drunk drivers, cover-ups, knowing safety violations.

  • DUI and street racing cases
  • Knowing safety violations
  • Concealed defects or hazards
  • Gross corporate negligence

Deadlines

The clock is already running.

California's statutes of limitations are unforgiving. Miss the deadline and your claim is barred forever — no matter how strong it is.

2 years
Most personal injury claimsCA Code of Civil Procedure § 335.1

Car accidents, slip & fall, motorcycle and pedestrian collisions, premises liability. The clock starts on the date of the injury.

6 months
Claims against government entitiesGov. Code § 911.2

City buses, dangerous public roads, county and state property. An administrative claim must be filed first — missing it bars the lawsuit entirely.

2 years
Wrongful death claimsCA Code of Civil Procedure § 335.1

Measured from the date of death. Eligible family members include the surviving spouse, domestic partner, and children.

Don't measure your deadline yourself. Exceptions, discovery rules, and claims involving minors or multiple defendants can shorten or extend these windows. The only safe move is to have an attorney calendar your specific deadlines — which we do, free, in your first consultation.
