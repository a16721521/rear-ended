DUI accident

Most car accident cases cannot pursue punitive damages. DUI cases can.

California Civil Code § 3294 allows punitive damages when a defendant's conduct is malicious, oppressive, or fraudulent — or constitutes "conscious disregard" for the safety of others. Courts have consistently held that choosing to drive drunk is conduct that meets this standard. This opens a category of damages unavailable in ordinary negligence cases, where compensation is capped to what was actually lost. In a DUI case, the jury can award additional amounts designed to punish the defendant and deter similar conduct.

DUI cases come with a built-in evidentiary advantage. A criminal DUI arrest typically produces a blood or breath alcohol test, a police report documenting impairment, field sobriety test records, and sometimes dashcam or bodycam footage. A criminal conviction — even a plea to a lesser charge — is powerful evidence in the civil case. We monitor the criminal proceedings from day one and coordinate civil discovery with the criminal timeline to maximize the evidentiary record available to us.

California Business and Professions Code § 25602.1 creates a cause of action against commercial alcohol sellers who serve an "obviously intoxicated minor." Adult dram shop liability is more limited in California than in other states, but where the drunk driver was a minor served alcohol at a bar, restaurant, or event, a second defendant with commercial insurance may be available. We evaluate this in every DUI case.

DUI accident evidence moves fast. The driver's vehicle, blood test chain of custody, bar surveillance footage, and witness availability are all time-sensitive. The criminal case will move on its own timeline — often faster than the civil case. Contact us immediately — we preserve civil evidence while the criminal case proceeds.

Step-by-step

What to do after a DUI crash.

The actions you take in the hours and days after a DUI crash have an outsized effect on your recovery — both medical and financial. The criminal case moves quickly. So should you.

Free case review →
1
At the scene
Call 911 and tell them the driver appears impaired.

Officers arriving at the scene will administer field sobriety tests and potentially a preliminary alcohol screening test. This contemporaneous documentation is your single most valuable piece of evidence. Do not let the driver leave if you can safely prevent it by describing them to 911 dispatch.

2
At the hospital
Tell medical staff the other driver was suspected drunk.

Tell medical staff exactly what happened, including that you were hit by a suspected drunk driver. Emergency records that reflect the mechanism of injury and your initial statement create a medical-legal paper trail from hour one. Request a copy of all records, imaging, and treatment notes before discharge.

3
Within 24 hours
Get the police report number and arrest information.

The arresting officer's report will document field sobriety tests, BAC results (if taken at the scene), the driver's statements, witness accounts, and the officer's observations of impairment. In complex cases, we subpoena the full investigation file including dashcam and bodycam footage.

4
Before the driver's arraignment
File the civil lawsuit before the criminal case moves.

The criminal case begins moving quickly. We file a civil lawsuit promptly to trigger discovery rights and preserve evidence independent of the criminal case. Civil discovery can proceed in parallel with the criminal case — deposition of the driver is often postponed until after the criminal case resolves, but we begin document discovery and third-party discovery immediately.

5
As the criminal case proceeds
Track every development in the criminal proceedings.

Monitor every development: arraignment, bail hearings, plea offers, and trial date. A guilty plea to DUI is admissible in the civil case. A conviction at trial is conclusive proof of negligence per se under California law. We track the criminal case and time civil proceedings to capture maximum evidentiary benefit.

6
At settlement or trial
Build the punitive damages case with precision.

Punitive damages require clear and convincing evidence of malice, oppression, or conscious disregard. We build this through the DUI investigation records, the driver's BAC level, any prior DUI history, and expert testimony on the dangerousness of driving at the measured impairment level. Insurance policies typically do not cover punitive damages — which means they must be collected from the defendant personally, an important consideration in settlement strategy.

Common injuries

What DUI crashes
actually do.

DUI collisions are disproportionately high-energy — wrong-way impacts, red-light runners, freeway stops ignored entirely. The injuries reflect that severity, and the punitive damages overlay raises the stakes on every one.

Traumatic Brain Injury
DUI crashes are disproportionately high-speed, often involving wrong-way drivers, red-light runners, or freeway stops ignored entirely. High-energy impacts produce TBI at higher rates than low-speed collisions. The punitive damages overlay means the stakes for TBI in a DUI case are significantly higher than in standard crash litigation.
Spinal Cord Injury
High-speed DUI collisions are a leading cause of traumatic SCI. Combined with the punitive damages exposure in DUI cases, spinal cord injury claims against drunk drivers can produce the highest verdicts in California personal injury law. Life care planning and forensic economic analysis are essential to these cases.
Wrongful Death
DUI is the leading cause of traffic fatalities in California. Wrongful death actions against drunk drivers include all survival damages, pre-death pain and suffering, and — critically — punitive damages for the family's benefit. We handle wrongful death DUI cases with a dedicated expert team from day one.
Orthopedic Fractures
Multiple fractures, comminuted fractures, and joint destruction are common in DUI crash mechanisms. Surgical repair, hardware installation, and lengthy rehabilitation create substantial economic damages. Impairment of extremity function affects earning capacity in physically demanding occupations.
Internal Organ Injury
Abdominal impact, steering wheel compression, and seat belt injury can produce liver laceration, splenic rupture, bowel injury, and pneumothorax. These injuries require emergency surgical intervention and extended ICU stays. Associated complications — sepsis, blood loss anemia, prolonged ventilation — add to damages.
Psychological Trauma
Victims of DUI crashes — crashes caused by someone who chose to drive impaired — report elevated rates of PTSD, chronic anxiety, and phobia of vehicle travel compared to victims of ordinary crashes. The deliberate nature of the risk-taking by the drunk driver is a recognized aggravating factor in psychological harm. These non-economic damages are fully compensable and are often undervalued in initial settlement offers.
What they don't want you to know

The playbook they run on DUI victims.

Insurance adjusters and defense teams follow a coordinated strategy in DUI cases. The stakes are higher — and so are the tactics used against you.

Tactic 01
Racing to Policy Limits Before Punitive Damages Are Assessed
Insurers often move fast to offer policy limits in DUI cases before a punitive damages demand crystallizes. Policy limits generally do not cover punitive damages — so once limits are tendered, the only remaining source for punitive recovery is the defendant personally. We evaluate the defendant's personal assets before advising on any policy limits settlement to ensure you're not giving up punitive exposure that has real collectability.
Tactic 02
The "One-Time Mistake" Narrative
Defense teams humanize drunk drivers: first offense, remorseful, going through a difficult time. This narrative is designed to undercut punitive damages by arguing the conduct doesn't rise to "malice" or "conscious disregard." California courts have rejected this argument consistently — the conscious disregard standard is met by the decision to drive impaired, not by the driver's subsequent attitude.
Tactic 03
Attacking BAC Test Validity
Breath test results are attacked on calibration, chain of custody, and timing grounds. Blood test results face similar challenges. We retain toxicological experts who can defend the reliability of the prosecution's BAC evidence in the civil case and, where appropriate, establish impairment through behavioral observation evidence independent of the chemical test.
Tactic 04
Minimizing Non-Economic Damages
Insurers routinely lowball pain, suffering, and emotional distress in DUI cases by framing them against standard crash benchmarks — ignoring the specific psychological harm of being victimized by someone who chose to drive drunk. We present PTSD diagnoses, treating therapist records, and expert testimony on the elevated psychological impact of DUI victimization to counter this minimization.

Time limit

Two years to file. The criminal case moves faster — we move with it.

California Code of Civil Procedure § 335.1 gives you two years from the date of injury to file a personal injury lawsuit. But the practical reason to act immediately is the criminal case: plea negotiations, evidence disclosure, and the defendant's statements are happening now. We file civil proceedings immediately, coordinate with the criminal timeline, and ensure the evidentiary record from the DUI arrest is fully preserved for your civil case. If a government vehicle or road defect was involved, the deadline is six months under Government Code § 911.2.
