Hit-and-run accidents

The driver is gone. Your right to compensation is not.

Los Angeles has one of the highest hit-and-run rates of any city in the United States. Roughly 20,000 hit-and-run crashes are reported in California annually — and many more go unreported because victims assume they have no recourse without an identified driver. That assumption is wrong.

California Insurance Code § 11580.2 requires every auto liability policy issued in the state to include uninsured motorist (UM) coverage unless the policyholder explicitly waives it in writing. If the driver who hit you fled the scene and is never found — or is found but has no insurance — your own UM coverage steps in as the primary source of recovery for medical bills, lost wages, and pain and suffering.

Beyond UM coverage, we investigate to identify the fleeing driver. Surveillance footage from adjacent businesses, traffic and red light cameras, paint transfer forensics, license plate fragment witnesses, and social media tracking have all produced identifications in cases that initially looked unsolvable. When a driver is identified, we pursue them through civil litigation regardless of whether criminal charges are filed.

We also examine third-party liability: the bar or restaurant that over-served an intoxicated driver, an employer whose employee was driving a company vehicle, or a vehicle owner who entrusted a car to a reckless driver. Multiple recovery paths, pursued simultaneously, are the Law Dog approach. Start your case today — evidence disappears fast.

Uninsured motorist coverage

Your policy is probably your best source of recovery.

UM coverage applies in three distinct scenarios — and most clients don't know they have it or how to use it.

Scenario 01
Driver fled and was never identified
The classic hit-and-run. Your UM coverage treats the unknown driver as uninsured and pays your damages up to your policy limit. You must file a police report — most policies require this as a condition of UM coverage for hit-and-runs. We make sure the report is filed correctly and your claim is documented from day one.
Scenario 02
Driver was identified but has no insurance
California requires minimum insurance, but millions of drivers aren't compliant. If the at-fault driver is found but uninsured, your UM coverage steps in — and we simultaneously pursue the driver personally through civil litigation. A judgment against an uninsured driver earns 10% annual interest and can be enforced against wages, bank accounts, and property.
Scenario 03
Driver's policy limits are too low for your injuries
Underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage — often bundled with UM — pays the gap between the at-fault driver's policy limit and your actual damages. If your medical bills exceed their $15K minimum policy, your UIM coverage covers the difference up to your limit. This is the most underused coverage in California and often the most valuable.

Step-by-step

What to do after a hit-and-run.

The actions you take in the first hour after a hit-and-run determine how much evidence survives. Surveillance cameras are still recording. Witnesses are still at the scene. Every minute matters.

Free case review →
1
Immediately
Do not chase the fleeing driver.

It's dangerous, it's illegal to pursue at speed, and it leaves the scene — which can complicate your own legal position. Note everything you can from a stationary position: make, model, color, any part of the license plate, direction of travel, and any distinctive features of the vehicle.

2
Immediately
Call 911 and report everything about the other vehicle.

Give dispatch every detail you have: direction the driver fled, vehicle description, plate fragments. A police report is not just documentation — most UM policies require a police report as a condition of coverage for hit-and-runs. Make sure the report is filed before you leave the scene.

3
At the scene
Collect every witness before they leave.

Bystanders often see more than the victim — partial plates, the direction fled, the driver's appearance. Get names and phone numbers from everyone present. Offer to text them immediately so they have your number. Witnesses who don't come forward at the scene rarely come forward later.

4
At the scene
Photograph everything, including paint transfer.

Your vehicle damage, the road surface, debris, and especially any paint transfer on your car. Paint from the fleeing vehicle can be forensically analyzed to identify the manufacturer, model-year range, and color code of the other car — narrowing the field for investigators dramatically.

5
Within 24 hours
Call us before notifying your own insurer.

Your own insurer handles UM claims, and they have financial interests that are not fully aligned with yours. Before you give any recorded statement or accept their timeline, talk to us first. We identify the UM notification requirements in your specific policy and ensure you comply without inadvertently harming your claim.

6
Within 24 hours
Seek medical attention, even if you feel okay.

Soft-tissue injuries, concussions, and internal injuries are frequently delayed in onset after the adrenaline of the crash wears off. A same-day or next-day medical visit creates the documentation that ties your injuries to this crash — and that documentation is the foundation of your UM claim.

What they don't want you to know

How your own insurer fights UM claims — and how we counter.

In a UM claim, your own insurer is the one writing the check — and their financial incentive is the same as any other insurer: pay as little as possible.

Tactic 01
Claiming UM Was Waived
Insurers sometimes claim UM coverage doesn't apply because it was waived when the policy was signed. Under California law, waiver requires a written, signed rejection form that specifically describes UM coverage and its limits. Generic fine print doesn't qualify. We audit your policy documents to determine whether any purported waiver is legally valid.
Tactic 02
Delaying Pending "Police Resolution"
Insurers use the open criminal investigation as a reason to stall the UM claim: "We're waiting to see if the driver is found." California law imposes timelines on UM claim handling regardless of the criminal investigation's status. We enforce those timelines through regulatory complaints and litigation if necessary — delay is not a defense.
Tactic 03
Disputing Third-Party Contact
Some policies require physical contact between the fleeing vehicle and yours as a condition of UM coverage for hit-and-runs — the "phantom vehicle" exclusion. California has specific rules on how this contact can be proven. If contact is disputed, witness corroboration and physical evidence become critical — another reason to document everything at the scene.
Tactic 04
Low-Ball UM Valuation
UM claims are handled by the same adjusters trained to minimize payouts — except now it's your own carrier doing it. They apply the same tactics: disputing medical necessity, arguing pre-existing conditions, and offering fast low settlements before your treatment is complete. A UM claim requires the same adversarial approach as a claim against any other insurer. We bring it.

Time limit

Two years — but your UM policy may have a shorter deadline.

California law gives you two years to file a personal injury lawsuit (CCP § 335.1). But your UM policy is a contract with its own notice and arbitration deadlines — often 30 to 60 days to notify your insurer and separate deadlines to demand arbitration. Missing a policy deadline can bar your UM claim entirely, independent of the civil statute of limitations.
